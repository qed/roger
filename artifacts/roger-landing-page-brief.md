## 1. Brand: mastery and excellence

### The idea
Roger is personal intelligence built like a master chef runs a kitchen: everything prepared before you need it, done to a high standard, and better every week. The payoff is time back for the people and work that matter, and money saved along the way.

### Brand pillars
1. **Mise en place** ("everything in its place"): the chef's discipline of prepping before service. Roger has the week, the inbox, and the meeting ready before you walk in.
2. **The toque** (the chef's white hat): legend says its 100 pleats stand for 100 ways a master can cook an egg. It stands for range and mastery, never gimmicks.
3. **Holding the bar**: work is done to a standard. Roger never invents numbers, facts or doctrine.
4. **Kaizen**: small improvements every week. Each swap, edit and approval teaches it.

### Taglines (pick one or A/B test)
- "Mastery, in everything you hand off."
- "Mise en place for your life."
- "Everything prepared. Nothing dropped."
- Home: "Dinner's planned. Groceries are ordered. You've got your Sunday back."

### Voice
Calm, precise, quietly confident, like a great maître d'. Short sentences and concrete numbers. No hype, no robots, no sparkles, no "AI-powered" in big type.

### Visual language
- Symbol: a minimal toque line icon (with visible pleats) as the logo mark placeholder.
- Palette: chef's-white / warm cream background, deep ink navy or charcoal text, one accent (copper or brass, like kitchen pans).
- Type: a refined serif for headlines (editorial, Michelin-guide feel), clean sans for body.
- Imagery: craft and preparation: a laid-out mise en place, a tidy pass, hands at work, a family table. Use placeholders (Unsplash with credits).
- Layout: generous whitespace, fine rules and hairlines, numbered steps like a menu or a recipe card. Subtle motion only (fade-up); respect prefers-reduced-motion.

## 2. Product in one line
Personal intelligence that prepares and runs the routine work of your home and your job, to a master's standard, and gives you back the hours.

## 3. Audiences (two doors on one page)
- **Home:** busy parents, usually the household's default planner. First product: weekly meal planning and grocery shopping.
- **Work:** founders, executives and operators drowning in email, meetings and tools. Product: a Chief of Staff plus a team of specialist agents.
- Launch market: Canada (Toronto first). Canadian spelling (favourite, colour), CAD.

## 4. The promise
**Time back, money back, done right.** Make every claim concrete: minutes, hours, dollars.
- Home: meal planning, lists and shopping take about 2-3 hours a week. With Roger, about 10 minutes.
- Home: the average Canadian household throws out over $1,300 of edible food a year (National Zero Waste Council, 2022). Planning around ingredients used 2-3 times attacks that.
- Work: the inbox, meeting follow-up and status-chasing are handled before you start your day.

## 5. Page goal and CTA
- Primary CTA: **Join the waitlist** (email required; optional "For: Home / Work / Both", postal code).
- Secondary CTAs: "See a week at home" and "See a day at work" (scroll to each section).
- Repeat the primary CTA in the hero, after each audience section, and in the footer.

## 6. Page sections, in order

### 6.1 Hero
- Headline: "Mastery, in everything you hand off."
- Subhead: "Roger prepares your week like a chef preps a kitchen: dinners planned and groceries ordered at home, inbox drafted and meetings followed up at work. You get the hours back."
- Toggle or two buttons: **For home** | **For work**.
- CTA: email field + "Join the waitlist".
- Visual: toque mark + a phone mockup showing a Sunday meal message alongside a morning Chief of Staff brief.

### 6.2 The problem (3 short cards)
- "What's for dinner?" asked every night.
- An inbox that's a to-do list somebody else wrote.
- Decisions made in meetings that never reach the tools where work happens.

### 6.3 Mise en place: how Roger works (3 steps, recipe-card style)
1. **Prepares:** reads your calendar, inbox, tasks and history, and gets everything ready.
2. **Proposes:** brings you the plan (15 dinners, a set of drafted replies, today's open loops). You approve in minutes.
3. **Executes and learns:** does the work, logs what it's blocked on, and gets better every week.

### 6.4 Roger at home: weekly meal planning (first product)
Four numbered steps:
1. **Sunday: 15 ideas.** Built around your family's tastes, this week's sales, and what's in the pantry.
2. **You pick 5-7.** About 5 minutes. Easy meals land on your busiest nights, because Roger reads the family calendar (soccer practice means a 20-minute dinner).
3. **Roger shops.** Builds the online grocery order with the brands and sizes you actually buy, double-checks pantry staples, adds the kids' usual snacks.
4. **Recipes arrive.** A clean PDF of the week's recipes in your inbox. Glance at the cart, pick a pickup window, done.

Feature tiles (6):
- **Knows your calendar:** easy meals on busy nights.
- **Cooks like a chef:** each ingredient used 2-3 ways, so less waste and a lower bill.
- **Knows your brands:** preferred brands, flavours and sizes.
- **Shops smart:** follows your rules ("only buy berries on sale," "cherry or grape tomatoes, either is fine").
- **Family recipe book:** winning meals come back into rotation.
- **Pays for itself:** a weekly receipt of hours and dollars saved.

Time math band: "2-3 hours → 10 minutes" and "Up to 150 hours a year back with your family."

### 6.5 Roger at work: your Chief of Staff and specialist team
Intro line: "A Chief of Staff who runs the day before you do, and a specialist for every lane of your work."
Present as five groups, each a card with a heading and 2-4 one-line items. Tool names are text only, no logos.

**A. Chief of Staff: the day starts handled**
1. **Morning brief before 7am:** pulls open loops from email and tasks, and suggests what it can knock out before you start.
2. **Every email starts as a draft:** scans the inbox about every 2 hours and drafts replies to nearly everything, checking your calendar for availability and finding the requested attachments and links.
3. **One task list that talks back:** Todoist as the central list, where Roger logs what it's blocked on, plus items from meetings and email. Paste a link into chat to tell it how to solve one.

**B. Meetings that turn into action**
4. **Prep briefs before you walk in:** pulled from meeting notes (Granola) and your docs (Notion).
5. **Decisions don't rot in notes:** after every meeting, decisions are folded into Notion, Linear and Todoist.
6. **Live capture to action:** recordings (e.g. Plaud) become notes the agents can actually act on.

**C. A specialist for every lane**
7. **Specialist agents:** one each for curriculum, engineering, coaching, hiring, content and ops, plus one for every piece of software you use.
8. **Group rooms:** 2-4 agents share one project thread, so you stop copy-pasting context between them.
9. **Mentor Mind:** a coach that helps you hold the bar, grounded in your own standards, never invented doctrine.

**D. Work that runs while you're offline**
10. **Always-on routines:** e.g. monitors app errors (Sentry) and proactively fixes them.
11. **Coding agents that ship:** cloud agents pick up Linear issues, run the open-work list by you at end of day, open PRs, then squash-merge to main when done.
12. **No collisions:** engineering work is tracked automatically in Linear, so your agents and the product team's agents don't step on each other.

**E. Knowledge and output, on demand**
13. **A living knowledge base:** a shareable Notion workspace plus a GitHub repo, updated daily from what actually happened.
14. **Honest progress look-ups:** ask anytime where a student, client or project stands, pulled across your systems without inventing numbers.
15. **Presentations without slide tools:** decks spun up in Gamma or Claude Design.

Band under the groups: "Works with Gmail, Google Calendar, Notion, Linear, Todoist, Granola, GitHub, Sentry, Gamma, Plaud, and more." (text only)

### 6.8 FAQ (accordion)
- **Do I still approve everything?** Yes. Roger drafts and prepares; nothing is sent, ordered or merged without your OK unless you set a rule that it can.
- **Home: do I need a grocery delivery account?** You'll check out on your grocer's site or app; Roger builds the cart.
- **Home: what if we don't have years of order history?** A quick taste quiz plus your past grocery receipts gets Roger to know your family within a couple of Sundays.
- **Home: allergies and picky eaters?** Set hard noes for each family member; Roger never breaks them.
- **Work: which tools does it connect to?** Email, calendar, Notion, Linear, Todoist, meeting recorders, GitHub and more.
- **Is my data private?** Your data is used only to do your work. It is never sold.
- **What does it cost?** Pricing is coming. Waitlist members get early access.

## 7. Technical requirements
- Single page. Static stack (plain HTML/CSS/JS, or Next.js/Astro if preferred). Deployable to Vercel or Netlify.
- Mobile-first, fully responsive; Lighthouse 90+ on performance and accessibility.
- Home/Work toggle in the hero scrolls to or highlights the matching section; state saved to the waitlist form.
- Waitlist form: client-side email validation; POST to a configurable endpoint (env var `WAITLIST_ENDPOINT`). Default to a simple serverless function or Formspree placeholder. Inline success: "You're on the list. We'll be in touch."
- Capture UTM parameters and the Home/Work choice in hidden fields.
- SEO: title "Roger: Personal intelligence for home and work", meta description, Open Graph image placeholder.
- Accessibility: semantic headings, alt text, visible focus states, AA contrast.
- Analytics hook placeholder (Plausible or GA4), off by default.
- Config file for product name, taglines, CTA copy, endpoint, and accent colour.

## 8. Out of scope
- No login, pricing table, payments, or real integrations.
- No invented testimonials, logos, user counts or results.
- No third-party logos (tool names as plain text only).
