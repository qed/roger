> **For the coding agent.** This file upgrades the existing Roger landing page (Vite + React + TypeScript + Tailwind + framer-motion, generated by Magic Patterns) from a **waitlist for a software product** into a site that **sells one product**: the **$2,000 work setup**. A **$500 home setup** lives on its own page. **Free workshops** are the front door, on a separate page that hosts are sent to.
>
> Read the whole file before you start. Build in the order in §11. Sections marked **TODO(Peter)** need real inputs from Peter. Build them as config-driven slots that **hide themselves when empty**, and track every one in the launch checklist (§8.4), which production deploys enforce. Never ship invented numbers, testimonials or logos.

---

## 0. The one-paragraph brief

Roger is a Toronto concierge for personal AI. **Peter** personally sets businesses up with AI assistants on **their own accounts**. He picks the right assistant (vendor-neutral: e.g. Muse, Grok Bot, Dots, Claude), connects it, and gets a Chief of Staff plus 3 helpers doing real work within 5 days. **Roger is the service, not the bot.**

The site has **one job: make it easy to say yes to the work setup.** The main page sells only that product, with one primary action (**Book a free fit call**) and one shortcut (**Skip the call: pay the deposit**). Everything else lives on its own page:
- `/home` for the $500 home setup
- `/workshops` for BIA and association hosts
- `/library` for browsing real-world use cases

Money is collected with **Stripe Payment Links on Peter's own Stripe account**. **Cal.com** is used only for free scheduling and never touches money.

## 3. The offer (source of truth; put it in `src/data/offers.ts`)

### 3.1 The product: Work setup, $2,000 CAD founding price ($3,000 after the first 10 clients)
- **What you get:**
  - a **Chief of Staff assistant** for the owner (§4.1) plus **3 helper assistants** picked from the menu (§4.2)
  - set up on the business's own accounts and connected to its email, calendar and tools
  - a **setup report** with before/after numbers (§3.6)
  - 30 days of email support
  - *Founding bonus:* a 60-day tune-up session
- **How:**
  - free 20-min fit call **within 1 business day**
  - if it's a fit, the deposit is paid and Session 1 booked **during the call**
  - Session 1 **within 2 business days**: the first brief and drafts **within 1 hour**
  - Session 2: helpers live
  - **fully live within 5 days**
  - remote by default; in person in Toronto on request
- **Payment:** $1,000 to book; $1,000 after a full week of running. (After the founding 10: $1,500 / $1,500.)
- **Guarantee:** "If your setup isn't working within 14 days of Session 1, I refund the $1,000 too. You pay nothing." The second half is only due once it runs.
- **Two doors in:**
  1. **Book a free fit call** (primary, Cal.com) → Peter pastes the deposit link into the call chat → `/thanks/work` → Session 1 booked before hanging up.
  2. **Skip the call** → the $1,000 deposit Payment Link on the page → `/thanks/work` → book Session 1. The fit check is the first 15 min of Session 1; full refund if it's not a fit.
- **Balance:** Peter emails the $1,000 balance link after the week of running. That link is never on the site.
- **Effort for Peter:** about 2.5 half-days per client.

### 3.2 Secondary: Home setup, $500 CAD founding price ($750 after the first 10 clients), on `/home` only
- **What you get:** your first assistant, chosen for you and set up on your own account, doing **5 jobs you pick** (§4.3). Plus a setup report, a day-7 check-in, 30 days of email support and the founding 60-day tune-up.
- **How:** one 90-min video session (in person in Toronto on request), **within 3 business days of payment** (TODO(Peter): confirm). Live the same day.
- **Guarantee:** full $500 refund if it's not working within 14 days of the session.
- **Flow:** **Pay $500 & book** (Payment Link) → `/thanks/home` → book the session (picks prefilled).
- **Effort for Peter:** about 1 half-day.

### 3.3 Front door: Free workshop, on `/workshops` only
- **For:** BIAs, business associations, chambers, school and parent communities. 10–40 people. 60–90 min, in person in Toronto or on Zoom.
- **Format:** Peter sets up one real assistant live, start to finish.
- **Attendees leave with:**
  - a one-page starter checklist
  - a 14-day code: $250 off the work deposit, or $100 off home
  - a QR code to `/?code={GROUP}-WORK`
- **The ask at the end:** "If you want this in your business, book a fit call this week."

### 3.4 Founding 10, capacity, and the path to $10K
- **Founding clients:** the first 10 paid setups (work or home) get founding prices plus the 60-day tune-up.
  - Copy: "Founding price: $2,000. Becomes $3,000 after the first 10 clients."
  - **Never show a struck-through "regular" price.** Nobody has paid $3,000 yet, so a strikethrough would present a reference price that was never charged. Canada's Competition Act restricts misleading "regular price" claims. State the future price as a future price, and honour it. (Not legal advice; Peter to confirm.)
  - TODO(Peter): confirm the 60-day tune-up as the founding perk.
- **Capacity:** "I take 3 setups a week." When `spotsLeft` hits 0: "Founding spots are full. Regular price from here: $3,000." The CTAs keep working at the new price; Peter swaps the Payment Links.
- **The $10K plan (for Peter; not shown on the page): 5 work setups.**

  | Source | Fit calls | Close (~40%) | Notes |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 3 pilots → 2 intros each | 6 | ~2 | Ask every pilot for 2 introductions at the setup-report handover |
  | 2 workshops booked pre-launch × ~20 attendees | ~4 | ~2 | Workshop code + "book this week" ask |
  | BIA newsletter + X posts | ~3 | ~1 | Every issue ends with the fit-call link |
  | **Total** | **~13** | **~5 = $10,000** | Home sales are upside, not in the plan |

  - **Load:**
    - 5 setups × 2.5 = 12.5 half-days
    - 13 fit calls ≈ 1.5
    - 2 workshops ≈ 2
    - **≈ 16 half-days over about 6 weeks**
  - **Launch gates** (the site's paid CTAs don't go live until all are true):
    1. 3 pilot case studies in `src/data/caseStudies.ts`.
    2. 2 workshops booked on the calendar.
    3. Every Stripe and Cal link is set.
    4. Terms/Refunds reviewed.
  - **Weekly scorecard** (Peter, Monday, from Stripe + Cal + analytics; a simple sheet):
    - fit calls booked / held
    - deposits (call vs. skip-the-call)
    - setups live
    - balances paid
    - refunds
    - workshop requests
    - top traffic sources

### 3.5 What "working" means (the guarantee's definition)
- **Working** = every helper/job the client picked has **run on its own at least once and produced output the client would use**.
- It's checked together at the end of the week of running (work) or at day 7 (home), and recorded in the setup report.
- **If Peter and the client disagree, the client decides.** That line is deliberate: it's what makes the guarantee believable.
- Windows: 14 days from Session 1 (work) or from the session (home).
- Claim: one email to `contactEmail`. Refund to the original card through Stripe within 10 business days.
- When a refund happens, the client keeps their accounts. Peter removes his access.

### 3.6 Pilots and the setup report (the proof engine)
**Pilots (before launch):**
- 3 setups, **at least 2 of them businesses**. Free or discounted; Peter decides case by case.
- Each pilot agrees in writing to:
  - be named (person + business)
  - give a photo or logo
  - give a 1–2 sentence quote
  - publish a before/after measurement
  - make **2 introductions** to other business owners
- Pilots don't use up founding spots.

**Setup report (every client, pilots included):**
- A 1–2 page document at the end of week 1.
- Contents:
  - the assistant chosen and why
  - the helpers/jobs set up
  - the rules (what it may do without asking)
  - a **baseline** measured at the fit call
  - the **week-1 result**
  - next steps
- Example measures: time to reply to a customer; overdue invoices; hours on admin per week; emails needing the owner each morning.
- A redacted pilot report is published at `/public/sample-setup-report.pdf`: "See exactly what you get."

**Effort:** 3 pilots ≈ 4–6 half-days of Peter's time before launch, in parallel with the build.

#### 8.4.4 `npm run launch-check`
- `scripts/launch-check.ts`, run with `tsx` (add it as a **dev** dependency; it's the only other addition beyond `react-router-dom`).
- Prints a table grouped by Blocking / Not blocking / Optional, with ✅ / ❌ and the label. Ends with: `Launch check: 14/27 done · 6 blocking`.
- Flags:
  - `--write` writes `src/generated/launch-status.json` (gitignored)
  - `--strict` exits with code 1 if any blocking item is ❌
- `package.json`:
  ```json
  "launch-check": "tsx scripts/launch-check.ts",
  "prebuild": "tsx scripts/launch-check.ts --write"
  ```

#### 8.4.5 Production deploys fail until launch-ready
- In `prebuild`, if `process.env.VERCEL_ENV === 'production'`, run in `--strict` mode. Any blocking ❌ fails the build. Vercel then refuses the production deploy (and keeps the last good production deploy live, if there is one).
- **Preview and local builds never fail** on the checklist. They only report. Peter can review the site at any time.
- The failure message lists exactly which blocking items are missing.

#### 8.4.6 Preview-only banner and `/launch` page
- `vite.config.ts` exposes the environment: `define: { __VERCEL_ENV__: JSON.stringify(process.env.VERCEL_ENV ?? 'development') }`.
- When `__VERCEL_ENV__ !== 'production'`:
  - a slim fixed strip at the top of every page reads "Launch check: 14/27 · 6 blocking →", linking to `/launch`
  - the `/launch` route renders the full checklist from `launch-status.json`, grouped as in the script output, each item with its label, kind and status
- In production builds, neither the banner nor the `/launch` route exists. Both are tree-shaken out, and the route isn't registered.

#### 8.4.7 Relationship to the rest of the spec
- §8.1's empty-value behaviour still applies. Missing items hide or show "Opening soon" on previews.
- The checklist is the **single source of truth** for "is it done?". The README links to `npm run launch-check` and doesn't keep its own TODO list.
- Adding a new TODO later means adding a line item here. If a TODO isn't in `launch.ts`, it doesn't exist.
